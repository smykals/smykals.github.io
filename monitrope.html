MONITROPE

A network monitoring appliance that discovers devices, learns what normal looks like for each one, and explains outages by tracing the probable cause.

At a Glance

The problem: Conventional monitoring can overwhelm an operator with device-down alerts without showing whether multiple symptoms share one upstream cause.

The engineering approach: Combine device discovery, topology, telemetry, behavior baselines, event correlation and explainable incident evidence in a self-hosted appliance.

Current evidence: A v0.6.0 release, an installable appliance distribution, eight containerized services and 305 passing backend tests at release. End-to-end performance on real lab incidents has not yet been established.

Why I Built It

Most monitoring tells you that something is down. I wanted a tool that also says why, the way I troubleshoot on the plant floor: understand normal behavior first, then trace a failure back through what depends on what. MONITROPE is that idea turned into software.

What It Does

  • Discovers and Identifies Devices

    Builds an inventory of what is on the network and maps how devices link together.

  • Learns Normal Behavior

    Watches each device over a learning period and builds a baseline of what normal looks like.

  • Detects and Explains Problems

    Flags behavior that departs from the baseline and traces outages to a probable cause instead of reporting every downstream symptom.

  • Notifies and Records

    Sends alerts by push, email, webhook or browser, keeps history for replaying incidents, and produces a report of the evidence behind its conclusions.

  • Answers Questions

    Lets you ask about your network in plain language.

Implementation and validation status vary by feature; this page describes the design as released.

Engineering Decisions

Self-hosted deployment: Keep the monitoring stack on infrastructure controlled by the operator, with a Docker Compose path and a dedicated Debian-based appliance installation option.

Separate services: Divide API, background work, monitoring, learning and analysis responsibilities so the system can be tested and developed in distinct components.

Behavior before conclusions: Use observed device history and relationships to inform investigations rather than treating every alert as an independent failure. The intended learning window is seven days; accuracy still requires real-world validation.

Controlled updates: Use a signed release manifest with integrity checks so an appliance can verify what it is installing.

From Alerts to an Investigation

A useful monitoring system should distinguish the symptom from the likely upstream cause. If a shared network device fails, multiple downstream devices may stop responding. The design goal is to preserve the individual observations while presenting them as one related investigation.

What Is Proven, and What Still Needs Testing

Demonstrated at release: v0.6.0 packaging and release artifacts, signed-update workflow implementation, containerized architecture, and 305 passing automated backend tests.

Not yet demonstrated publicly: Accuracy of learned baselines over a full observation window, reliability of root-cause conclusions during actual network faults, and long-running performance on dedicated lab hardware.

OT applicability: Industrial-network monitoring is an area for future evaluation, not a claim that MONITROPE has already been deployed or validated in an operational technology lab.
