Bags Weighed, but Never Labeled

An automated line weighed every bag, but labels never printed. I proved the network healthy, so the right team could fix the real fault: a weighing sensor.

Environment
Continuous-production plant
My role
IT engineer on site
Worked with
Electrical engineering, operations
Outcome
Sensor replaced, labeling resumed

The Problem

Bags moved down an automated line and were weighed correctly, but the labeling step never completed. Labels matter on a production line: product without them can't ship, so the line was effectively stopped at that step.

Because the labeler and the PLC both sit on the plant network, the first question was whether the network had broken the conversation between them.

What I Did

  1. Checked the Switch Port

    Confirmed the port serving the PLC was up, with no errors or flapping. Healthy.

  2. Checked the VLAN Configuration

    Confirmed the port was in the expected VLAN and the PLC could reach the labeler. Healthy.

  3. Verified Communication and Control Signals

    Worked with electrical engineering to confirm the PLC was communicating and sending the signals we expected. Healthy.

  4. Followed the Dependency Upstream

    With the network and PLC ruled out, the missing input pointed to the weighing sensor. Faulty, and replaced.

Why It Matters

On a plant floor, every team can see the same symptom. The fastest fix comes from proving, step by step, which layer is not at fault, so the right specialist works on the right part. Isolating the network quickly kept IT from becoming the bottleneck and kept the investigation moving.
