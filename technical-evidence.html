Building-to-Building Fiber Failure

Impact
Intermittent connectivity affected infrastructure between buildings.
My investigation
I scoped the affected area, compared the impacted systems for common dependencies, isolated network segments, and ruled out endpoints and switching where appropriate. This narrowed the failure domain to the physical network path.
Finding
The fiber connection was identified as the failure point. The available account does not specify the diagnostic readings or test equipment used to confirm it.
Restoration
A temporary Ethernet connection restored connectivity while the permanent fiber repair was coordinated. My documented contribution is the impact assessment and fault isolation; installation and repair ownership are not specified.
Observed outcome and limits
Connectivity was restored through the temporary path. The account does not record the recovery checks, monitoring duration, downtime, or completion of the permanent repair.
Technical judgment
Narrow the failure domain before changing configurations, restore service through a workable temporary path, and distinguish restoration from permanent resolution.

07 / Working methodology

Recurring Failure and Root Cause Analysis

My approach
Restoring service does not necessarily resolve the underlying problem. For recurring incidents, I look for patterns in timing, affected equipment, network paths, environmental conditions, configuration changes, vendor activity, logs, and previous tickets.
Investigation sequence
Symptom → Evidence → Common dependency → Root cause → Corrective action → Validation → Documentation
What this demonstrates
Troubleshooting discipline, documentation, reliability improvement, and a Green Belt approach to recurring problems.

08 / Working approach and professional boundaries

Troubleshooting Technology Outside My Specialty

Context
Site IT touches PLCs, controls systems, production applications, specialized equipment, vendor systems, and industrial platforms. IT is not always the subject-matter expert for the equipment itself.
My contribution
I can establish whether an endpoint has link, validate addressing, test the network path, inspect VLAN, routing, and firewall behavior, determine whether other devices are affected, identify where communication stops, and collect evidence.
Escalation boundary
When the issue crosses into control logic or a vendor-specific domain, I bring in Controls or the vendor with the findings. Infrastructure troubleshooting does not imply PLC programming expertise.
What this demonstrates
Technical ownership, useful escalation, and a clear understanding of the limits of my specialty.

02 / Technical scenario

Production Line Down: PLC or Network?

Scenario
One production line is down while the rest of the facility remains operational. Maintenance suspects the PLC, Controls suspects the network, and a contractor recently worked in the affected area.
How I would investigate
I would establish the failure timeline, determine which devices and communications are affected, identify their common network path, inspect the area where work occurred, and test connectivity from both sides of the suspected boundary.
Decision discipline
Recent physical work makes damaged fiber, Ethernet, power, or another physical dependency a hypothesis to test. It does not establish the cause.
What this illustrates
IT/OT fault isolation, production support, evidence-led diagnosis, and cross-functional communication.
03 / Technical scenario informed by network-support experience

VLAN and Network Connectivity Failure

Scenario
A group of systems loses access while other areas continue operating normally.
How I would investigate
I would determine whether the affected systems share a switch, VLAN, subnet, uplink, gateway, DHCP scope, DNS dependency, firewall path, or application dependency.
Path to isolate
Endpoint → Access port → VLAN → Trunk → Switching → Gateway → Firewall/routing → Destination
Decision discipline
Each test should eliminate part of the path. I would use the findings to guide the next test rather than change configurations until something works.
What this illustrates
Cisco switching, VLANs, 802.1Q, routing, DHCP/DNS, firewall troubleshooting, and change discipline.
04 / Technical and leadership scenario

IT Security and OT Availability

Scenario
A security requirement calls for a change to an OT-connected system, while Operations or Controls identifies potential production risk.
How I would evaluate it
I would understand the vulnerability and actual exposure, then assess operational dependencies, vendor support requirements, maintenance windows, rollback capability, and consequences of failure.
Controlled response
If immediate remediation cannot be performed safely, I would evaluate segmentation, access restrictions, monitoring, or isolation as compensating controls while developing a controlled remediation plan with the responsible teams.
What this illustrates
Risk assessment, OT security thinking, stakeholder coordination, segmentation, and balancing security with availability.
05 / Technical scenario informed by production-support experience

Major Production Outage

Scenario
Several production-impacting problems are reported simultaneously.
Prioritization
Safety → Production impact → Scope → Business impact → Workaround availability
How I would coordinate
I would establish incident ownership, identify symptoms that might share a dependency, assign parallel investigation where possible, and keep Operations informed while troubleshooting continues.
Restoration and follow-through
Restore service safely, then investigate the cause and the actions needed to prevent recurrence.
What this illustrates
Incident management, escalation, prioritization, communication, and technical leadership.
06 / Technical scenario

Emergency Change or Wait?

Scenario
A production-impacting issue has a possible infrastructure fix that would require a change outside the normal maintenance window.
Decision question
Is the risk of changing it now lower than the risk of leaving production in its current state?
How I would evaluate it
I would assess impact, confidence in the diagnosis, dependencies, rollback capability, available subject-matter experts, operational approval, and whether a temporary workaround could safely bridge the gap until a maintenance window.
What this illustrates
Risk-based change decisions, rollback planning, production awareness, and operational judgment.
