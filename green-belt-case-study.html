01 / Problem & My Role

One Support Process, Uneven Outcomes.

Label-printing incidents were handled inconsistently, with unclear ownership, variation in troubleshooting and escalation, higher ticket volume, and operational disruption.

My role: I led the Green Belt improvement project and development of standardized support instructions, clarified ownership and escalation through a RACI, improved ticket visibility and aging controls, and supported documented operator and supervisor training.

Context: Manufacturing IT/OT support. The employer and internal systems are anonymized.

02 / Analysis

The Average Exposed the Gap. Variation Explained It.

Application-issue mean time to resolution was 191.47 hours against a 72-hour resolution-time target. A median of 14.44 hours and maximum of 1,937.72 hours showed that extreme cases heavily influenced the average.

03 / Work Performed

Turn the Findings into a Repeatable Support Process.

Standard troubleshootingLed development of instructions covering mechanical, application, software, network, and communication issues.
Ownership & escalationClarified roles and escalation responsibilities through a RACI, making the support path more explicit.
Ticket visibilityImproved open-ticket visibility and aging controls so unresolved issues could be tracked and escalated.
Station identificationAdded easier IP identification at printer and PC stations to support troubleshooting.
Training & knowledge transferSupported documented operator and supervisor training alongside the standard instructions.
Data-informed reviewUsed DMAIC, VOC, SIPOC, Pareto analysis, and MTTR/variation analysis to connect incident patterns with process changes.
04 / Measured Outcome

Fewer Tickets During the Improvement Period.

Monthly ticket volume declined from 6.3 to 2.3 during the improvement period. This is the measured outcome documented in the project summary.

Measurement context: The 72-hour resolution-time goal was a target. The available summary does not establish that it was achieved, provide the comparison-period lengths or ticket counts, or demonstrate that the ticket reduction persisted after the improvement period.
05 / Lessons & Control

Improve the Process the Team Can Control.

The project reinforced the value of looking beyond a single average. Variation revealed where waiting, ownership, escalation, and knowledge gaps influenced the support experience.

Standard work, explicit responsibilities, ticket visibility, and training gave the team a more defined support process. The result demonstrates how technical support and process improvement can work together.
