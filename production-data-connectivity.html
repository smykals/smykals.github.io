The Challenge

Manufacturing information systems tell a plant what to make, record what happened, and print what ships. When one becomes unreachable, the cause can sit anywhere between the operator's screen and the server, and each layer usually belongs to a different team.

The Dependency Chain

  1. Operator stationWhere the problem is reported
  2. →
  3. Plant networkSwitching, wireless, VLANs
  4. →
  5. Windows serversHosting and services
  6. →
  7. ApplicationsSAP workflows, PI Vision, labeling

I work the chain from left to right, ruling each layer in or out.

Diagnostic Workflow

  • Investigate Connectivity and Availability

    Identify the scope: one station, one area or a shared service. Capture the symptoms and what operators can reproduce.

  • Trace Application Dependencies

    Test relevant switching, wireless, addressing, name resolution, server availability and application dependencies as appropriate. Separate confirmed evidence from assumptions.

  • Coordinate with Owners

    Share the affected scope, tests performed and observed results with application owners and other technical teams so investigation continues at the correct layer.

  • Restore Access

    Support corrective action within the responsible team's scope, then verify with operators that the affected workflow is available again.

Evidence and Outcome

This page describes a recurring support method. It does not document a single dated incident, confirmed failed component, restoration time or quantified downtime reduction. Those details have not been invented.

For a specific production incident with a documented diagnostic sequence, identified cause and resolution, read Bags Weighed, but Never Labeled. The two pages demonstrate different skills: a reusable operational workflow here and incident-level fault isolation there.
