Professional IT/OT fault isolation

Bags weighed, but never labeled

An automated line weighed every bag, but the labeling step never completed, so product could not ship. I proved the network healthy, layer by layer, so the right team could fix the real fault: a weighing sensor.

My role
Owned the network and connectivity checks as the sole onsite IT engineer. Electrical engineering verified the PLC signals with me.
Constraint
Every team could see the same symptom, and the PLC, labeler and sensor belong to other specialists.
Decision
Prove each layer before changing anything, instead of adjusting network configuration on a guess.
Outcome
Sensor replaced, labeling resumed. Downtime duration and dates are not recorded.

Professional Continuous improvement

Making label-printing support consistent

Label-printing incidents were handled differently every time, with unclear ownership and escalation. I led a Lean Six Sigma Green Belt project that standardized troubleshooting, clarified who owns what, and trained the people closest to the printers.

My role
Led the project and the support instructions. Delivery involved the support and operations teams.
Constraint
Resolution time also depended on application complexity, specialist availability and waiting, which support could not control.
Decision
Focus on what the support process could change: visibility, ownership, troubleshooting, escalation, training and prevention.
Limits
Comparison-period lengths, raw counts and whether the drop held after the project are not established. The 72-hour resolution goal was a target, not a demonstrated result.
Read the full case study
01 / Identify recurring failures02 / Define ownership and escalation03 / Standardize troubleshooting04 / Train and review

What the baseline showed

Application-issue mean time to resolution was 191.47 hours against a 72-hour target, but the median was 14.44 hours. A few extreme cases, up to 1,937.72 hours, were skewing the average. Application issues were 41.8% of incidents and configuration issues 26.9%.

What changed

  • Standard troubleshooting instructions for mechanical, application, software, network and communication issues
  • A RACI for ownership and escalation
  • Open-ticket visibility and aging controls
  • Easier IP identification at printer and PC stations
  • Documented operator and supervisor training

MONITROPE

Self-hosted, private-first network monitoring that explains outages

Discovers devices inside approved scopes, learns each one's normal behavior, and traces outages to a probable cause instead of alerting on every downstream symptom. Runs as Docker Compose services, a Debian-based appliance image or a Windows desktop app, with signed one-button updates.

  • Recorded Oct 2026 Version 0.6.0 in my lab: installable OS image, Ed25519-signed updates, 305 automated backend tests passing, and discovery of 18 devices across eight approved-range scans. Learning and root-cause analysis were not yet proven at the time
  • Current, unreleased 0.8.0 Candidate 3 passed development validation on its release branch. Development testing only, not validation on a production network
  • Ongoing Discovery and device onboarding work, separate from the candidate. Root-cause analysis has not been proven against a real failure

My role: creator and technical lead. I used Claude to help draft code, layouts and interface text; I set the direction, made the technical decisions, and reviewed and tested what went into each build.
